FedCARE: Client-Adaptive Curriculum and Reference Evolution
for Federated Direct Preference Optimization
Anonymous Author(s)
Submission Id: SUBMISSION-ID
Abstract
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) aligns language models with
pairwise preferences, but its federated application must accom-
modate heterogeneous client distributions, unequal usefulness of
preference pairs, and changing personalized policies. We propose
FedCARE, a federated DPO framework that couples client-adaptive
curriculum construction with personalized reference refresh. Each
client ranks eligible preference pairs using a policy-dependent
margin: an initial-policy preference gap in the first round and a
change in that gap between successive personalized checkpoints in
later rounds. Under a Bradley–Terry clean-preference model and
bounded implicit-reward approximation error, we derive a margin-
dependent lower bound on preference validity and an upper bound
on the associated gradient discrepancy. After scoring and selection,
each client refreshes its reference from the current pre-update per-
sonalized checkpoint and freezes it throughout local DPO. We adopt
the selective LoRA-sharing mechanism of FedSA-LoRA to aggregate
LoRA-𝐴 while retaining LoRA-𝐵 locally. Under the reported evalu-
ation protocol,FedCAREachieves 70.26% macro-average accuracy
on MATH and 24.24 macro-average BLEU on CodeXGLUE, exceed-
ing the strongest compared baselines by 1.90 percentage points
and 3.47 BLEU points, respectively. Additional results under cross-
domain heterogeneity, varying client scales, and multiple backbone
models further demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of
FedCARE.

reasoning-intensive tasks such as mathematical problem solving,
code generation, and logical inference[6, 36]. Unlike general instruc-
tion following, these tasks require models to maintain coherent
intermediate steps, long-range consistency, and reliable solution
verification [22, 35]. Strengthening the reasoning ability of LLMs
has thus become a central goal of post-training and alignment.
Preference optimization offers an effective route to this goal.

Most preference optimization methods, however, assume that
all preference data are centrally available [3, 39]. This assumption
rarely holds for reasoning-oriented applications, where data are
split across clients, institutions, or domains under privacy and own-
ership constraints [18, 20]. Federated learning is a natural remedy,
but applying DPO directly in a federated setting raises three coupled
challenges, illustrated in Fig. 1. First, preference distributions are
heterogeneous: clients differ in reasoning domains, difficulty levels,
and preference criteria, so a single shared adapter cannot serve
them all [33]. Second, preference pairs are not equally useful: pairs
with large model-aware margins provide more reliable preference
signals, whereas low-margin or ambiguous pairs are more likely to
contain uncertain, noisy, or unstable supervision [8]. Third, a fixed
DPO reference can become stale as the personalized client policy
changes across communication rounds, leaving subsequent local
updates anchored to an earlier policy state [24]. This motivates a
client-specific reference refresh schedule that preserves the tempo-
ral comparison needed for curriculum scoring while updating the
reference before local optimization.
To address these challenges, we proposeFedCARE, a federated
DPO framework based on Client-Adaptive Curriculum and Refer-
ence Evolution for LLMs. In contrast to conventional federated DPO,
which trains on a fixed local preference set,FedCAREtreats curricu-
lum construction as a dynamic, client-specific process: each client
scores candidate preference pairs by their usefulness and reliability
