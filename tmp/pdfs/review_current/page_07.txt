4.3 Generalization and Robustness Analysis
Analysis of Cross-Domain Generalization.Table 3 reports Pass@1
performance under cross-domain federated heterogeneity.Fed-
CAREobtains the highest macro average in this protocol. However,
its margin over Local DPO is small, and it does not achieve the best
score on every individual task. We therefore interpret this result as
evidence of a favorable aggregate trade-off under this federation,
rather than uniform superiority across domains.
Analysis of Backbone Robustness.Table 4 shows thatFedCARE
consistently improves performance across different backbone mod-
els. The gains appear on Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math, Llama-3.2, and
Mistral, indicating that the proposed method is not tied to a spe-
cific base model family. Although the absolute performance varies
with the backbone capability, the consistent improvement suggests
that client-adaptive curriculum selection remains beneficial under
different initialization strengths.
Analysis of Client Scalability.Figure 5 evaluatesFedCAREwith
increasing client counts and different Dirichlet heterogeneity levels.
As the federation grows, accuracy decreases from 78.76% to 66.89%
in Figure 5(a), highlighting the challenges of learning across a larger
federation under the evaluated partitioning protocol. Nevertheless,
FedCAREremains applicable across the tested client scales, using
client-specific curriculum selection and selective LoRA sharing
without pooling client preference data. These results support its
applicability to larger federations while identifying performance
degradation with increasing scale as an area for further improve-
ment.
