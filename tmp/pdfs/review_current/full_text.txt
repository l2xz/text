FedCARE: Client-Adaptive Curriculum and Reference Evolution
for Federated Direct Preference Optimization
Anonymous Author(s)
Submission Id: SUBMISSION-ID
Abstract
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) aligns language models with
pairwise preferences, but its federated application must accom-
modate heterogeneous client distributions, unequal usefulness of
preference pairs, and changing personalized policies. We propose
FedCARE, a federated DPO framework that couples client-adaptive
curriculum construction with personalized reference refresh. Each
client ranks eligible preference pairs using a policy-dependent
margin: an initial-policy preference gap in the first round and a
change in that gap between successive personalized checkpoints in
later rounds. Under a Bradley–Terry clean-preference model and
bounded implicit-reward approximation error, we derive a margin-
dependent lower bound on preference validity and an upper bound
on the associated gradient discrepancy. After scoring and selection,
each client refreshes its reference from the current pre-update per-
sonalized checkpoint and freezes it throughout local DPO. We adopt
the selective LoRA-sharing mechanism of FedSA-LoRA to aggregate
LoRA-𝐴 while retaining LoRA-𝐵 locally. Under the reported evalu-
ation protocol,FedCAREachieves 70.26% macro-average accuracy
on MATH and 24.24 macro-average BLEU on CodeXGLUE, exceed-
ing the strongest compared baselines by 1.90 percentage points
and 3.47 BLEU points, respectively. Additional results under cross-
domain heterogeneity, varying client scales, and multiple backbone
models further demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of
FedCARE.

code generation, and logical inference[6, 36]. Unlike general instruc-

intermediate steps, long-range consistency, and reliable solution
verification [22, 35]. Strengthening the reasoning ability of LLMs
has thus become a central goal of post-training and alignment.
Preference optimization offers an effective route to this goal.
Rather than merely imitating reference answers, it trains the model
to assign higher likelihood to preferred responses than to rejected

Most preference optimization methods, however, assume that
all preference data are centrally available [3, 39]. This assumption
rarely holds for reasoning-oriented applications, where data are
split across clients, institutions, or domains under privacy and own-
ership constraints [18, 20]. Federated learning is a natural remedy,
but applying DPO directly in a federated setting raises three coupled
challenges, illustrated in Fig. 1. First, preference distributions are
heterogeneous: clients differ in reasoning domains, difficulty levels,
and preference criteria, so a single shared adapter cannot serve
them all [33]. Second, preference pairs are not equally useful: pairs
with large model-aware margins provide more reliable preference
signals, whereas low-margin or ambiguous pairs are more likely to
contain uncertain, noisy, or unstable supervision [8]. Third, a fixed
DPO reference can become stale as the personalized client policy
changes across communication rounds, leaving subsequent local
updates anchored to an earlier policy state [24]. This motivates a
client-specific reference refresh schedule that preserves the tempo-
ral comparison needed for curriculum scoring while updating the
reference before local optimization.
To address these challenges, we proposeFedCARE, a federated
DPO framework based on Client-Adaptive Curriculum and Refer-
ence Evolution for LLMs. In contrast to conventional federated DPO,
which trains on a fixed local preference set,FedCAREtreats curricu-
lum construction as a dynamic, client-specific process: each client

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We proposeFedCARE, a federated direct preference op-
timization framework that integrates FedSA-LoRA-style
personalization with client-adaptive curriculum selection
and an update-after-scoring personalized reference refresh
schedule.
• We design aClient-Adaptive Curriculum Constructionstrat-

2 Related Work
2.1 Federated Parameter-Efficient Adaptation
Federated learning (FL) lets clients train collaboratively without
sharing private data. FedAvg aggregates local updates on the server
[26], but suffers from client drift under non-IID data. FedProx intro-
duces proximal regularization to stabilize local optimization under
heterogeneous data [ 21], and recent surveys summarize hetero-
geneity, communication, privacy, and personalization as central FL
challenges [18]. Personalized FL mitigates client drift by balanc-
ing global sharing against local specialization [33]; representative
approaches include FedPer and FedRep, which separate shared
representations from client-specific components [ 1, 7]. Because
full-parameter federated fine-tuning is prohibitive for LLMs, a line
of work instead updates LoRA adapters over a frozen backbone
[14, 25, 40]. Most relevant to us, FedSA-LoRA observes that LoRA-A
and LoRA-B play asymmetric roles and shares only LoRA-A across
clients [11].FedCAREadopts this selective-sharing principle but,
unlike prior federated LoRA work aimed at instruction tuning,
integrates it with preference optimization and margin-driven cur-
riculum construction for LLMs.
2.2 Federated Preference Optimization
Preference optimization aligns LLMs with human or task-specific
preferences by favoring preferred responses over rejected ones.
RLHF first trains reward models from human comparisons and
then optimizes policies with reinforcement learning [ 5, 28, 32].
DPO removes the explicit reward model by optimizing pairwise
preferences through a reference-regularized objective [31]. Subse-
quent methods further analyze or simplify preference optimization,
such as IPO, KTO, and SimPO [2, 9, 27]. Recent studies begin to con-
nect alignment with federated learning. OpenFedLLM provides a
benchmark and framework for decentralized LLM fine-tuning [39].
FedCAREis complementary: rather than fixing the local preference
set, each client dynamically selects useful pairs based on its own
policy and reference model.
2.3 Curriculum Preference Data Selection
Curriculum learning improves training by ordering samples mean-
ingfully, typically from easy to hard [4]. Related ideas such as self-
paced learning and competence-based curricula further show that
adaptive data schedules can improve optimization stability [19, 30].
In preference optimization, difficulty-aware methods construct or
select preference pairs according to their learning signal. Curry-
DPO introduces ranked preferences through curriculum learning
[29], Less is More similarly shows that selecting high-quality pref-
erence data can improve alignment efficiency [8]. In federated set-
tings, FibecFed explores curriculum learning for LLM fine-tuning
under client heterogeneity [23]. Yet these methods either assume
centralized preference data or do not address DPO-style preference
optimization, which motivates our client-specific margin-driven
curriculum.

3.2 Method Overview

candidate scoring, curriculum selection, reference refresh, local
DPO, and LoRA-𝐴 aggregation. Each client first scores its available
preference pairs against the reference retained from the preceding
round and constructs a client-adaptive curriculum. Only after selec-
tion does DPRE refresh the reference from the current personalized
checkpoint. The client then optimizes its local policy against this
frozen reference and uploads the updated LoRA-𝐴 factor. Round 1
uses the base-policy preference gap for curriculum scoring, as spec-

4 Experiment
4.1 Experimental Setup
Datasets and Federated Partitions.We evaluateFedCAREon
heterogeneous mathematical reasoning and code-to-text gener-
ation benchmarks. The main mathematical reasoning experiments
use Hendrycks MATH [13], where different mathematical subjects
are treated as different clients. The code-to-text experiments use
CodeXGLUE code-to-text [16], where different programming lan-
guages correspond to different clients. We additionally use Meta-
MathQA [41] as the public mathematical preference pool for cur-
riculum construction. To further examine robustness, we consider
additional evaluation settings with GSM8K [6] and programming
challenge generation on APPS [12]. For client-number scalability,
we construct non-IID GSM8K partitions with different numbers of
clients and Dirichlet concentration parameters.
Baselines and Evaluation Metrics.We compareFedCAREwith
representative centralized, local, and federated preference-optimization
baselines, including the base model, Local DPO, FedIT[39], FedVA[39],
and FedSA-LoRA[11]. These baselines cover non-federated local
adaptation, standard federated instruction tuning, federated value
alignment, and selective LoRA aggregation. We use task-native eval-
uation metrics: Pass@1 for MATH, GSM8K and APPS, and BLEU for
CodeXGLUE code-to-text generation. Unless otherwise specified,
we report the macro average over clients or tasks.
Training Configuration.All experiments are conducted using
four NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPUs with DeepSpeed. Our training pipeline
is implemented based on OpenRLHF [15]. Qwen2.5-7B [34, 37] is
used as the default backbone, while Qwen2.5-Math-7B [38], Llama-
3.2-3B [10], and Mistral-7B [ 17] are used to evaluate backbone
generality. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is used during initial preference-
data construction, and mathematical reasoning is evaluated with
the official Qwen2.5-Math evaluation framework. All LoRA-based
methods use rank 𝑟= 64, scaling factor 𝛼= 128, and dropout0 .1.
Complete training and selection hyperparameters are provided in
the appendix.

4.3 Generalization and Robustness Analysis
Analysis of Cross-Domain Generalization.Table 3 reports Pass@1
performance under cross-domain federated heterogeneity.Fed-
CAREobtains the highest macro average in this protocol. However,
its margin over Local DPO is small, and it does not achieve the best
score on every individual task. We therefore interpret this result as
evidence of a favorable aggregate trade-off under this federation,
rather than uniform superiority across domains.
Analysis of Backbone Robustness.Table 4 shows thatFedCARE
consistently improves performance across different backbone mod-
els. The gains appear on Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math, Llama-3.2, and
Mistral, indicating that the proposed method is not tied to a spe-
cific base model family. Although the absolute performance varies
with the backbone capability, the consistent improvement suggests
that client-adaptive curriculum selection remains beneficial under
different initialization strengths.
Analysis of Client Scalability.Figure 5 evaluatesFedCAREwith
increasing client counts and different Dirichlet heterogeneity levels.
As the federation grows, accuracy decreases from 78.76% to 66.89%
in Figure 5(a), highlighting the challenges of learning across a larger
federation under the evaluated partitioning protocol. Nevertheless,
FedCAREremains applicable across the tested client scales, using
client-specific curriculum selection and selective LoRA sharing
without pooling client preference data. These results support its
applicability to larger federations while identifying performance
degradation with increasing scale as an area for further improve-
ment.

without curriculum filtering, which leads to the longest per-round
runtime and requires uploading the full LoRA adapter. In contrast,
FedCAREuses model-aware curriculum selection to train on a
smaller but more reliable subset, reducing the per-round training
time from 3h14m to 2h14m. Meanwhile, it uploads only LoRA-𝐴
and keeps LoRA-𝐵 client-local, resulting in the same communica-

aggregation. This shows that the proposed curriculum improves
data efficiency while preserving communication efficiency.
4.5 Privacy Considerations
FedCARE keeps preference data, personalized LoRA-𝐵 factors, and
reference checkpoints local, communicating only LoRA-𝐴 factors
for aggregation. This reduces direct exposure of client-specific in-
formation, although shared updates may still reveal information
about local data and do not provide a formal privacy guarantee. All
experiments use public datasets with simulated clients. Additional
safeguards, such as differential privacy or secure aggregation, could
be incorporated for privacy-sensitive deployments.
5 Conclusion
This paper introducesFedCARE, a federated DPO framework for
LLMs under heterogeneous client distributions.FedCAREbuilds
client-adaptive curriculum by selecting preference pairs according
to client’s evolving policy state, shares transferable LoRA-𝐴 factors
while keeping client-specific LoRA-𝐵 factors local, and refreshes
the personalized reference policy through DPRE. Experiments on
mathematical reasoning and code-to-text generation benchmarks
show thatFedCAREachieves the best average performance among
the compared methods, remains effective under cross-domain het-
erogeneity, and works well with simple FedAvg aggregation. These
results suggest that effective federated preference optimization re-
quires not only communication-efficient parameter sharing, but
also adaptive control over the preference signals introduced to each
client.
