Analysis of Key Components.Table 6 examines the contributions
of client-adaptive curriculum selection, Dual-LoRA personalization,
and DPRE. The complete framework achieves the highest average
performance on both benchmarks, and removing any component
reduces its performance. Random Subset and Fixed Curriculum un-
derperformFedCARE, supporting the benefit of adapting sample se-
lection to each client’s evolving policy state. Removing Dual-LoRA
reduces MATH accuracy by 3.03 points and CodeXGLUE BLEU
by 8.54, highlighting the importance of personalized adaptation.
Removing DPRE yields corresponding decreases of 1.10 accuracy
points and 4.11 BLEU, supporting the utility of the personalized
reference-refresh schedule. Together, these results support inte-
grating adaptive selection, personalization, and reference evolution
within federated preference optimization.
Analysis of Efficiency and Communication.Table 7 compares the
computation and communication costs of different training strate-
gies. Full Data trains on the complete available preference pool
without curriculum filtering, which leads to the longest per-round
runtime and requires uploading the full LoRA adapter. In contrast,
FedCAREuses model-aware curriculum selection to train on a
smaller but more reliable subset, reducing the per-round training
time from 3h14m to 2h14m. Meanwhile, it uploads only LoRA-𝐴
and keeps LoRA-𝐵 client-local, resulting in the same communica-
tion cost as FedSA and much lower upload size than full-LoRA
aggregation. This shows that the proposed curriculum improves
data efficiency while preserving communication efficiency.
4.5 Privacy Considerations
FedCARE keeps preference data, personalized LoRA-𝐵 factors, and
reference checkpoints local, communicating only LoRA-𝐴 factors
for aggregation. This reduces direct exposure of client-specific in-
formation, although shared updates may still reveal information
about local data and do not provide a formal privacy guarantee. All
experiments use public datasets with simulated clients. Additional
safeguards, such as differential privacy or secure aggregation, could
be incorporated for privacy-sensitive deployments.
5 Conclusion
This paper introducesFedCARE, a federated DPO framework for
LLMs under heterogeneous client distributions.FedCAREbuilds
client-adaptive curriculum by selecting preference pairs according
to client’s evolving policy state, shares transferable LoRA-𝐴 factors
while keeping client-specific LoRA-𝐵 factors local, and refreshes
the personalized reference policy through DPRE. Experiments on
mathematical reasoning and code-to-text generation benchmarks
show thatFedCAREachieves the best average performance among
the compared methods, remains effective under cross-domain het-
erogeneity, and works well with simple FedAvg aggregation. These
results suggest that effective federated preference optimization re-
quires not only communication-efficient parameter sharing, but
also adaptive control over the preference signals introduced to each
client.
