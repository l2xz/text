Selection and Reporting Protocol.FedCAREselects curriculum
data by greedily ranking eligible preference pairs under the round-
specific budget and public-data constraints. To ensure a controlled
comparison, we fix the seeds used for tie-breaking and data order-
ing across methods; Random Subset is the only variant in which
candidate membership is sampled randomly. During evaluation,
all models use greedy decoding with temperature0, making the
reported scores deterministic for a fixed trained checkpoint. Unless
otherwise specified, we report checkpoint-level results under this
fixed protocol, and all comparisons are interpreted under the same
training budget, data split, and evaluation setting.
4.2 Overall Performance
Performance Analysis.Tables 1 and 2 show thatFedCAREim-
proves federated preference optimization across both mathemati-
cal reasoning and code-to-text generation. On MATH, our method
achieves the best average accuracy of 70.26%, exceeding the strongest
baseline by 1.90 accuracy points. On CodeXGLUE, our method also
obtains the highest average BLEU score of 24.24, exceeding the
strongest baseline by 3.47 BLEU. These results indicate that client-
adaptive curriculum construction, Dual-LoRA personalization, and
DPRE are effective across different heterogeneous tasks.
Analysis of Curriculum Evolution.Figure 3 illustrates the selected
curriculum across communication rounds on MATH. Candidate
availability follows a staged expansion from easy to medium and
eligible local hard pairs. Within each stage, however, clients greedily
reselect samples according to their own margin distributions and
reliability gates. The adaptive component therefore lies in client-
specific within-stage selection, rather than in learning the difficulty-
availability schedule itself.
Analysis of Communication-Round Dynamics.Figure 4 shows the
performance gain over the initial model under the same commu-
nication budget. After three rounds,FedCAREachieves gains of
10.95 accuracy points on MATH and 12.96 BLEU on CodeXGLUE,
exceeding the gains of the compared baselines. These results demon-
strate the effectiveness ofFedCAREin improving mathematical
reasoning and code-to-text generation over the evaluated commu-
nication rounds. The favorable gains under a fixed communication
